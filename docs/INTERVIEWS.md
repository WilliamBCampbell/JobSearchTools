# Interview preparation

Read the current invitation and calendar event to confirm employer, role, interviewer, stage, time zone, duration and format. Use the invitation for access details rather than copying meeting credentials into artifacts. A recruiter screen, technical interview and final leadership conversation need different emphasis.

Read the official/saved job description, entire career library, submitted package and previous interview debriefs. Do not assume every submitted sentence was confirmed. Correct outdated facts in rehearsal notes without silently changing historical application files.

Create a short PDF/HTML sheet, usually three to five readable pages:

1. Meeting details, a speakable introduction and why this role/company fits the candidate's direction.
2. Selected real stories with problem, personal action, result and likely follow-ups. Include older career evidence when it is the strongest match.
3. Role-specific scenarios with proposed approaches clearly distinguished from past achievements. Include honest ways to explain material gaps.
4. Company/product context connected to customer problems, thoughtful questions and a concise close.

Use verified public product information and explain why it matters. A price, launch date or feature without context is trivia. Do not invent interviewer interests, agendas or customer outcomes. If the candidate has never handled a scenario, prepare a hypothetical approach rather than a fictional success story.

Useful questions include what success looks like, where implementations fail, how teams resolve competing commitments, how training works, and what the interviewer enjoys about working there. Ask a personal question naturally; avoid combining three questions without giving them room to answer.

Map career claims and source employer facts. Render and inspect every page. Keep the interview copy easy to scan; detailed source limits can live in accompanying notes. Afterward, ask for a debrief, record next steps and update genuine new career facts with provenance. Send thank-you messages only with explicit authorization.
